# Triggers

Settled in [#191](https://github.com/ian-pascoe/cloudpid/issues/191). Each section says which slice builds it ([_Slices_](#slices)). S3 ([#680](https://github.com/ian-pascoe/cloudpid/issues/680)) built the Workspace trigger. S4 ([#681](https://github.com/ian-pascoe/cloudpid/issues/681)) built the Session trigger. S1 ([#678](https://github.com/ian-pascoe/cloudpid/issues/678)) landed in three parts: the engine (the Schedules, the Registry's definitions and `admitFire`, the Session's `fire`, the Trigger object and the Environment cascade), the tools with Approval, and the Settings section with Fire now and the chips. _As built_ notes say where what was built differs from the design.

A **Trigger** sends a message on its own when something happens: its Schedule comes due, or its Trigger URL gets a delivery. The owner sets one up, or the agent does for the owner. Triggers fire from a Durable Object of their own, the Registry keeps their definitions, and making or widening one waits for Approval ([ADR-0047](../adr/0047-triggers-fire-from-a-durable-object-of-their-own-the-registry-keeps-their-definitions-and-making-or-widening-one-waits-for-approval.md)). Trigger URLs sit outside Access behind a secret token and a required signature ([ADR-0048](../adr/0048-trigger-urls-sit-outside-access-behind-a-secret-token-and-a-required-signature.md)). GitHub triggers are repo webhooks the GitHub App makes ([ADR-0049](../adr/0049-github-triggers-are-repo-webhooks-the-github-app-makes.md)). The platform facts behind the design (GitHub's deliveries, Durable Object alarms, Access bypass, cron libraries, size limits) are in [`docs/research/triggers.md`](../research/triggers.md).

- **In #191:** Schedules and webhooks as sources; the three kinds of Trigger; the `github` and `standard` formats; repo hooks the GitHub App makes; the agent's tools; the Settings page's Triggers section; fire records and Notifications.
- **Later, separate issues:** a Slack format (it signs `v0:<timestamp>:<body>` and needs a `url_verification` handshake and a 3 s answer), a Linear format (`Linear-Signature`), one-off and delayed Schedules ("in 2 h, check CI") on Session triggers, a Resource watch as a source (ADR-0032 left starting a Run to Triggers), organization hooks, a per-Trigger switch for Notifications, and the trigger tools on the MCP endpoint. Archiving idle Sessions is [#675](https://github.com/ian-pascoe/cloudpid/issues/675), and releasing idle Workspaces whose work is pushed is [#676](https://github.com/ian-pascoe/cloudpid/issues/676).

## Kinds and what a Fire delivers to

A Trigger is one of three kinds, by what keeps it. Its kind fixes what each Fire does.

| Kind                    | Kept by        | Each Fire                                                                           | Goes when                                    | Slice |
| ----------------------- | -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ----- |
| **Environment trigger** | an Environment | starts a new Session in a new Workspace of it (the spare Workspace when current)    | its Environment is deleted                   | S1    |
| **Workspace trigger**   | one Workspace  | starts a new Session in that Workspace, on its files, branch and Container          | its Workspace is removed, or its Environment | S3    |
| **Session trigger**     | one Session    | sends its Trigger message to that Session's root conversation, on its active branch | its Session is deleted                       | S4    |

- **Environment trigger.** The Fire's Session is registered as `session_start` registers one (`createSession` with `workspace: "new"`), so it takes the Environment's spare Workspace and counts as its use, which keeps the spare being made again. The branch is always `createSession`'s default, `cloudpid/<workspace id>`: a fixed name would make every second Fire fail with `branch-taken`. A Trigger may hold a `base` (else each Repo's default branch), a `model` and a `thinking` level (else the Environment's Settings at the time of the Fire); it holds no Role, which is for delegating. The Default Environment may hold one, with Repos or none ("every morning, summarize X"). An Environment trigger never reuses a Session; that is a Session trigger's job. `environment_delete`'s Approval card counts the Environment's Triggers.
- **Workspace trigger.** The Fire's Session is registered with `workspace: <id>`, so it works on the Workspace's branch; the Trigger holds a `model` and `thinking`, and no `base` and no Role. **It keeps its Workspace:** the Registry deletes a Workspace record only while no Session and no Workspace trigger is bound to it. Deleting the last Session of a Workspace that has a Workspace trigger leaves the Workspace (the confirm dialog says "The Workspace stays: trigger `<name>` keeps it"), and the Settings page's Workspaces section lists it with no Session. Removing its last Workspace trigger does not remove the Workspace: it stays, with no Session and no Trigger, until the owner removes it, since it may hold unpushed work. The owner's **Remove** of the Workspace removes its Triggers too, the confirm naming them, and so does deleting its Environment. A Subagent Workspace (it lasts as long as its subagent, and subagents have no trigger tools) and a spare Workspace (bound to nothing) can't hold one; the Registry refuses both. This rule also settles a contradiction: `workspace.md` → Workspaces says a deleted Session leaves its Workspace until the owner removes it, while `client-and-protocol.md` → Delete and GLOSSARY → Deleted Session say the last Session's delete removes the Workspace. The second is the rule; S3 fixed `workspace.md` and added the Trigger exception to all three.
- **Session trigger.** The Fire's message goes to the root conversation's active branch, as `session_send`'s does, and the conversation carries on with its history; a move in the Session tree doesn't end the Trigger. While the Session is archived each Fire is skipped and recorded `archived`, and while a Session import is in progress `importing`; nothing needs resuming afterwards. A Fork or Clone doesn't copy the Session's Triggers (two Sessions would answer every Fire); the messages already sent stay in the copy's history. A Session trigger never outlives its Session, and so never keeps a Workspace.
- **Who may hold one.** Only the root conversation's agent makes Triggers; subagents can't ([_Agent tools and Approval_](#agent-tools-and-approval)). A Session trigger always sends to the root conversation.
- **The Session a Fire starts** (Environment and Workspace triggers) is titled by cloudpid, "`<trigger name>` · `<event>` · `<date, time>`", the time in the Trigger's zone and the event for webhooks only. The title is never generated (the payload would drive the title call, and Fires would get near-identical titles anyway); the owner can rename it.
- **Spending, as built in S4.** Before a Session trigger's Fire is sent, the Session checks its Spend limit (`SessionHost.overLimit`, the gate `startSessionHost` also uses) and fails the Fire for good, `failed: This Session is out of budget: <reason>. Raise or clear the spend limit in the settings to go on.` (the words the Session's own refusal uses), without starting anything.
- **Spending.** A Session a Trigger started is held to `spendLimit.perSession` like any other. A Session trigger's Fires add up in its one Session, so once that Session is at its Spend limit each Fire fails at no cost and pushes `failed`.

## Schedules

_Built in S1._

- **Syntax:** 5-field cron with names (`MON`, `JAN`) and the `@hourly`, `@daily` and `@weekly` shortcuts. 6- and 7-field forms are refused, as are the `L`, `W` and `#` extensions. There are no one-off or interval Schedules in #191.
- **Time zone:** each Schedule has a required IANA zone. The tool has no default; its description tells the agent to ask the owner, or to use a zone the Instructions name, since the agent can't know the owner's zone ("named, never assumed": [_Agent changes_](agent-changes.md)). The owner's form pre-fills the browser's zone. Defaulting to UTC was rejected: it would fire "Monday 9am" at the wrong hour without a word.
- **Daylight saving time:** croner's rules, as they are, with nothing wrapped around them. For a fixed-time expression (a single value in its minute and hour fields, such as `30 2 * * *`), a time skipped when clocks go forward fires once, at the first valid moment after it, and a time repeated when they go back fires once, at its first occurrence. For an interval expression (a wildcard, step, range or list in its minute or hour field, such as `*/15 * * * *`), a repeated time fires on both occurrences and a skipped time is skipped, as Vixie cron does.
- **Library:** croner (no dependencies, IANA zones, explicit DST rules) is the choice, provided a workerd test shows it loads and fires there; `cron-schedule`, already in `bun.lock`, has no zones, and `cron-parser` pulls in Luxon.
- **Interval:** no minimum beyond cron's own minute. Overlap, `wait` and coalescing bound what each Fire starts ([_Overlap_](#overlap)).
- **Missed Fires** (an outage, alarms that came late): one catch-up Fire, for the latest missed time, however late; any other missed times are recorded `missed`. The Fire's header shows the scheduled and the actual time. A Paused trigger never catches up on resume.
- **The alarm** is armed for the next Fire after each one. Alarms are at-least-once and their lateness is undocumented, so a Fire is not promised to the second; S6 measures it on a deployed stage.
- **As built** (`packages/protocol/src/trigger-schedule.ts`, over croner 11 in its 5-field mode): `cronProblem`, `zoneProblem` and `scheduleProblem` say why a Schedule is refused, `nextFires` lists the next times and `dueFires` what is due since the object's cursor (the catch-up Fire and the `missed` times, at most a fire log's worth, `MAX_FIRE_LOG`). Besides `L`, `W` and `#`, croner's `?` and `+` are refused too. Its Node tests pin the DST rules above; the Trigger object's workerd tests show croner loads and fires there.

## Webhooks

_Built in S2, in four parts ([#679](https://github.com/ian-pascoe/cloudpid/issues/679)): the source, the pure checks and the Session's payload (part 1); the delivery path, Trigger URLs and secrets (part 2); the agent's tools (part 3); the owner's form (part 4). All four are built; notes marked "part 1" to "part 4" say which part built what._

### The Trigger URL

- **Shape:** `<origin>/hooks/<trigger id>/<token>`, the id a UUIDv7 and the token 256 random bits as 43 base64url characters. The origin is the stage's, as the client document's is ([ADR-0035](../adr/0035-cloudpid-identifies-itself-with-a-public-client-document-and-takes-authorization-responses-outside-access.md)).
- **Routing:** the main Worker's exact-shape router matches the path before the Access guard, as `serveSignInPaths` does for the sign-in paths, and hands the request straight to `trigger:<id>`; every other form under `/hooks` (another shape, a trailing slash, other capitalisation) answers 404. Its constants live in a light module, as `mcp/sign-in-origin.ts` does for the sign-in paths, so the startup budget is kept ([`workers.md`](../development/workers.md)).
- **An unknown id** reaches an object with no storage, which answers 404 and writes nothing. A Trigger object's constructor is light, unlike a Session's, so this is cheap.

### Formats

Each webhook Trigger names one format. Every delivery must be signed; there is no unsigned mode.

- **`github`:** `X-Hub-Signature-256`, `sha256=` and the HMAC-SHA256 hex digest of the body, keyed with the Signing secret. The event is `X-GitHub-Event` (and the body's `action`); repeats are deduped by `X-GitHub-Delivery`. A `ping` is answered and shown on the Trigger's page as "GitHub reached this trigger"; it never fires.
- **`standard`:** [Standard Webhooks](https://www.standardwebhooks.com/)' `webhook-id`, `webhook-timestamp` and `webhook-signature`, with the spec's 5-minute timestamp window; repeats are deduped by `webhook-id`.

- **As built, part 1:** the router (`apps/server/src/hooks-routes.ts`, its constants in the light `hooks-path.ts`) matches the exact shape, `/hooks/<UUIDv7 in lowercase>/<43 base64url characters>`, case-sensitively against the path as it was sent; it answers `refused` (a 404) for every other form a lenient router would read as under `/hooks` (it reuses the sign-in router's `canonicalPath`) and leaves the bare `/hooks` to the guard. The signature checks (`apps/server/src/trigger/signature.ts`, over Web Crypto; GitHub's is compared by `verify`, which doesn't leak where it differs, and a `standard` header that lists several entries makes one HMAC over the body and compares each entry with that digest, so a header of thousands of entries costs one pass over a 25 MiB body): `github` is `sha256=<hex>` over the bytes received, keyed with the Signing secret as UTF-8; `standard` is any `v1,<base64>` entry of `webhook-signature` over `<id>.<timestamp>.<body>`, and a timestamp more than 5 minutes either way is `stale` whatever its signature, so a replay never looks signed. A `standard` Trigger shows its Signing secret as `whsec_<base64>` (`standardSecretText`), the form Standard Webhooks' libraries decode back to the same bytes.

- **As built, part 2:** `serveHooks` (`apps/server/src/hooks-serve.ts`) runs between the Access guard and the sign-in router (`guardedRoutes`'s third argument): a POST to the exact shape is read, at most 25 MiB (a body declared larger, or that goes over while read, is not held: the object gets its size and no bytes), and handed to `trigger:<id>`'s `deliver`, whose status (and no body, `Cache-Control: no-store`) is the answer; any other method is a 405, every other form under `/hooks` a 404, and an object that doesn't answer a 503. An unknown id reaches an object with no storage (`hasTriggerStore`), which answers 404 and writes nothing.

### Checks and answers

The Trigger object checks, in order, on the whole body in memory:

1. the token, by comparing its keyed hash with the kept one in constant time;
2. the signature, an HMAC over the bytes received, before anything is parsed (and, for `standard`, the timestamp window);
3. the size and content type;
4. the `match` filter ([_Filters_](#filters)) and, for `github`, the bot-loop guard.

| Case                                                                          | Answer | Kept                                           |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ | ---------------------------------------------- |
| unknown id, malformed path, wrong token                                       | 404    | counted per day (an unknown id writes nothing) |
| missing or bad signature, stale `standard` timestamp                          | 401    | counted per day                                |
| body over 25 MiB                                                              | 413    | counted per day                                |
| content type other than JSON, `application/x-www-form-urlencoded` or `text/*` | 415    | counted per day                                |
| GitHub `ping`                                                                 | 2xx    | its time, on the page                          |
| filtered out (by `match`, or the bot-loop guard)                              | 2xx    | counted per day                                |
| a bot's delivery while the App's own bot is not known (`github`)              | 2xx    | counted per day, as `bot_unverified`           |
| accepted                                                                      | 2xx    | a pending Fire, once it is stored              |

- **As built, part 2** (`deliver`, `apps/server/src/trigger/object.ts`; the pure reads in `delivery.ts`): the object never calls the Registry for a delivery it refuses or filters, and answers from the definition it caches. The token's hash is the vault's keyed digest (HMAC-SHA-256 under the key of the purpose `trigger:<id>`), compared in constant time. A body over 25 MiB was not read, so it gets the token check and then a 413: its signature cannot be checked. A GitHub `ping` is answered once its signature is good (before the content type is looked at), and its time kept; it never fires. The content types taken are `application/json` (and `application/*+json`), `application/x-www-form-urlencoded` and `text/*`; a body that is not JSON still fires (only a `match` path can then fail it). The sender's delivery id keyed the Fire (`<trigger id>:<delivery id>`, a hash of it when over 200 characters; a GitHub delivery with no `X-GitHub-Delivery` gets a random one), a repeat of one pending, or in the fire log within 24 hours, is answered 200 and makes no second Fire (a later one fires again, as the Registry's memory of the key ends too). A delivery to a paused Trigger is recorded `skipped: paused` at once. The answer comes after the pending Fire is stored; the object then sets its alarm for now.
- **25 MiB** is GitHub's own payload cap, so cloudpid never refuses a GitHub delivery for its size, and it fits one RPC call to the object (32 MiB).
- **The answer comes once the pending Fire is stored**, never after the Fire's Session starts: GitHub gives a delivery 10 s and does not redeliver on its own.
- **Refused and filtered deliveries are only counted per day**, never kept as rows, since anyone can make them; the counts are kept for 30 days. Only an accepted, matching delivery reaches the Registry, through `admitFire`.

### Secrets

- **Made by the Trigger object**, at creation: the token and the Signing secret, 256 bits each. They are sealed with the vault bound to the object's own id (as the Workspace DO seals its Secrets) and kept **only in the Trigger object**; the token is also kept as its keyed hash (the vault's HMAC-SHA-256 digest), which is what a delivery is compared with.
- **The agent never sees either**, as with Secrets. A tool result about a webhook Trigger points the owner to its page.
- **The owner reveals them** on the Trigger's page at any time (a pasted hook may need pasting again), and copies them.
- **Rotation:** the owner's **New URL** and **New signing secret** each apply at once; the old value stops working at that moment, with no window where both work. A hook cloudpid made on GitHub is updated in the same action ([_GitHub-managed hooks_](#github-managed-hooks)).
- **A reverted removal** brings the definition back with a new Trigger URL and Signing secret, and its Agent change says so. A GitHub-managed hook is made again on its own; a pasted URL must be pasted again, accepted because removal is a narrowing, as a kept Secret's deletion is.

- **As built, part 2:** the object makes both at its first adopt of a webhook definition that has none (`ensureSecrets`: a Schedule's object never does), as 43 base64url characters each, sealed with `openKeptVault(<master key>, "trigger:<id>")` bound to `{owner: <id>, name: "token" | "secret"}`, so one Trigger's sealed values open in no other. `trigger_secrets` holds the two sealed values and the token's keyed hash. The owner's revert of a removal wipes the object (`TriggerObjects.wipe`, tried as `wake` is) before it wakes it, and fails with a message saying the URL and secret may still be the old ones when it can't, so the Trigger it brings back makes new ones even when the removal's own sync never reached the object, which would otherwise still hold the removed Trigger's secrets. `reveal` and `rotate` make the secrets themselves when the object holds none (a creation that did not finish), and read the definition first when the object was never synced. A row of secrets that no longer reads is _unreadable_: deliveries to it are answered 404, counted `not_found` and logged as an error, `reveal` says so ("make new ones with New URL or New signing secret"), and only a rotation, which replaces both, mends it; nothing overwrites it silently. The object's `reveal` and `rotate("url" | "secret")` serve the owner's RPCs (`revealTrigger`, `rotateTrigger`: the path `/hooks/<id>/<token>`, the stage's origin when it has one, and the secret, `whsec_<base64>` for a `standard` Trigger); a rotation replaces the row at once, so the old value stops at that moment. A Trigger that is no webhook's has none, and the RPCs refuse it. The reverted removal's Agent change says so: "It has a new Trigger URL and Signing secret: paste them again where the old ones were used." The Agent tools (part 3) never carry either.

### Access

- **As built, part 2:** `hooksApplicationProps` (`apps/server/src/hooks-access.ts`), declared in `worker.ts` as `Server/Hooks` only while the Worker is live; the Worker's own Effect server span is turned off for requests under `/hooks` (`hooksTracing`, which sets Effect's `TracerDisabledWhen`; `hooksTelemetry` registers it through alchemy's `Telemetry.layer`, the one way a layer reaches a request's telemetry, since a plain provide on the init effect does not), since it records the whole URL, and the one span made records `/hooks/:id/:token` and the status.
- **`Server/Hooks`**, a fourth Access application on each deployed stage, has the one destination `<host>/hooks/*` and a Bypass/Everyone policy, with `domain` set to it and `adopt: true`, declared only while the Worker is live, as `Server/Bypass` is ([`infra.md`](../development/infra.md) → _Stages and Access_). ADR-0035's application stays as recorded.
- **Bare `/hooks`** stays behind Access (a `/hooks/*` path doesn't cover it), which is fine since nothing is served there.
- **`alchemy dev`** serves the route with no Access, so it can be tested with curl.
- **Previews:** CI's preview cleanup already deletes it, since its destination is a public one on the PR's host.

### Logs

- **The token is in the path**, so it lands in the owner's own Workers Logs (kept 7 days, readable only from the owner's account). GitHub's hooks can't send a custom header and a query string is logged too, so there is no better place; the HMAC is the real proof, and the Signing secret is never in a URL.
- **cloudpid's own spans and logs** record only the route template, `/hooks/:id/:token`, never the path. `hooks-serve.test.ts` runs a request through the composition alchemy's Worker bridge builds (the registration on the runtime context, `buildEventTelemetry`, Effect's server middleware) with a recording tracer and logger, and checks that neither the token nor the id is in a span or a log line. **The main Worker has no Effect tracer today** (no OTLP destination is bound), so Effect's server span is a native one nothing exports; the registration keeps it so when one is added. The Workers runtime's own fetch span and logs keep the URL, which is the accepted cost above.
- **Access logs nothing** on a bypassed path, so cloudpid's spans and the per-day counts are the only record of deliveries.
- **Rotation is the remedy** for a leaked URL.

## Payload and untrusted content

_The message's parts: S1 (header and Trigger message) and S2 (the body; part 2 builds what the object keeps and sends, part 4 the chip). GitHub trimming: S5._

A Fire's message has four parts, in order (the header comes first, as the S1 code writes it, so the message that follows is read as under it):

1. **A header cloudpid writes, first:** the Trigger's name and the Fire's time; for a Schedule, the scheduled and the actual time; for a webhook, the format, the event (`X-GitHub-Event` and `action`) and the delivery id; for a Workspace trigger's Fire that waited, how many more deliveries arrived meanwhile and to check the current state ([_Overlap_](#overlap)).
2. **The Trigger message, as written:** the owner's words (or the agent's, approved by the owner), never filled in from a payload. It may be as long as any input (1,900,000 bytes, `input_too_large`: [`mcp-endpoint.md`](mcp-endpoint.md) → Limits).
3. **The body, as untrusted content:** fenced after the owner's words, as an Attached resource's content is, and shown as a chip in the transcript. At most 16 KiB of it is inline, a bound on each Fire's context cost.
4. **The whole body, as a file:** the Session saves it to `.cloudpid/triggers/<fire key>.json` in its Workspace (the key made filename-safe, with a short hash of the whole key after it, so two keys never share a file) and names the path inside the fence, so the agent can run `jq` on it. The file holds the body **as received**, which the signature was made over: the object keeps the bytes, and the Session writes them as UTF-8 text, so a byte no UTF-8 text holds is a replacement character (U+FFFD) in the file (_as built_, below). A GitHub delivery with the content type `application/x-www-form-urlencoded` is `payload=<URL-encoded JSON>`: the file keeps that form, the fence's `content_type` says so, and `match` reads such a body as the JSON inside its `payload` field. The file is named `.json` whatever the content type.

- **Bodies up to 1 MiB are kept** whole, each pending Fire's in its own SQLite row (a Durable Object's row holds at most 2 MB). A larger body (up to 25 MiB) still makes a Fire, but its body isn't kept: the message says so, with its size, the event and the delivery id, and the agent reads the event through `gh` instead. Chunking and R2 were rejected for a rare case, and refusing with 413 would drop a real GitHub event, which GitHub doesn't send again.
- **As built, part 2:** the object keeps the body **as the bytes received** (the pending row's `body` BLOB, with the rest of the payload as JSON in `payload`), and the 1 MiB bound is on those bytes. It decodes them only to read them (`match`, the event, the sender: UTF-8, a byte order mark dropped) and when the Fire is sent, as UTF-8 with the byte order mark kept (`ignoreBOM`). `FirePayload.body` is a string, so a byte no UTF-8 text holds is a replacement character in what the Session writes to its file and shows: the file is the body as received for every body that is UTF-8, and the signature was checked over the bytes. A body over the bound is not kept, and its Fire carries `size` and no `body`; the pending row then keeps `facts`, what the Trigger's `match` read of it (each of its paths, nested), and `revision`, the revision of the definition whose `match` the delivery passed. The Fire is admitted at that revision; when the Registry answers `stale`, the delivery is checked again against the definition now current (its body, or its `facts`, for which a path not kept never matches), and one that no longer matches is counted `filtered` and dropped. A webhook Fire of a Workspace trigger that waits for its Workspace with later Fires merged into it is recorded `failed: it no longer matches the trigger's filter, N merged` instead, as a waiting Fire lost for good is, so the merged Fires leave a record. The last accepted delivery (its payload and body bytes) is kept in `trigger_hook`, and **Fire again** (`fireAgain`) enqueues `<trigger>:manual:<key>` with it, a manual Fire whose header says it was sent again. The Registry titles the Session with the event (`admitFire`'s `event`).
- **The last delivered body** (at most 1 MiB) is kept apart, for **Fire again** ([_The owner's UI_](#the-owners-ui)).
- **GitHub trimming** (S5, for the `github` format): `*_url` API links are dropped, except `html_url`, and the `repository`, `sender`, `organization` and `installation` objects shrink to their names. GitHub's payloads are mostly links.
- **As built, part 1:** the Session's `fire` takes the delivery as `FireRequest.payload` (`FirePayload`: format, event, action, delivery id, content type, size, and the body unless it was over 1 MiB). The block (`fencePayload`, `packages/protocol/src/trigger-payload.ts`) is `<trigger_payload>`, a notice that the body is untrusted, and one `<payload>` element with the sender's facts as attributes; the body is cleaned, redacted of the Secrets the Session holds, cut to 16 KiB at a character boundary and escaped, in that order, as an Attached resource's content is, and a sender's event, action, delivery id and content type are cut to plain characters once, where the Session's `fire` takes the payload in (`cleanPayload`), and the Registry does the same for the event its title carries; `truncated` is set only when the text shown is shorter than the body after cleaning and redaction. The Session writes the whole body to `/workspace/.cloudpid/triggers/<fire key>-<hash>.json` first (`host.ts`, `firePayload`, through the lookup environment, which never starts the Container), once and not again for a Fire it admitted before; a Workspace that can't be written fails the Fire as unavailable, which the object tries again. The block is a text part of its own after the message and the owner's entry keeps `trigger` (`TriggerPayloadChip`: where it came from, its size, how much went inline, its path) for the transcript's chip: the transcript takes the block out of the owner's message (`isPayloadBlock`) and carries the chip data as `triggerPayload`, as Attached resources are carried, and the transcript draws it (part 4, below). A body that was not kept has the block with `not_kept` and no file. A webhook's Session is titled "`<name>` · `<event>` · `<date, time> UTC`": a webhook has no zone, so the time is UTC and says so.
- **Rejected:** the whole body inline (it blows up the context and gives injected text the most room), and field templates in the Trigger message (they mix the payload into the trusted part, which is the injection hole).

## Filters

_Built in S2._ _As built, part 1:_ `match` lives on the webhook source, `{type: "webhook", format, match?}`, so it needs no column of its own and is part of what an Agent change records; `TriggerPatch.match` changes it alone (null takes it away; a Schedule's Trigger refuses one). A Trigger's source type and format are fixed at creation, like its kind and owner: the Registry refuses a patch that names another, a Schedule's patch replaces its source, and a webhook's source changes only through `match`. The owner's form (part 4) edits a webhook Trigger's `match` as rows and never sends a source for it. The evaluator is `matchesDelivery` (`packages/protocol/src/trigger-match.ts`), over the parsed body as JSON: a path is own keys joined by dots (an array's index too, `commits.0.message`), numbers and booleans compare as their text, an object, an array or nothing never matches, `event` is the delivery's event name before any body field of that name, and there is no cap on paths or values. `matchProblem` refuses a path with an empty part and a path that lists no values.

- **`match`:** a webhook Trigger may hold a `match`, a map from dotted paths in the body to lists of allowed values. Paths are ANDed; the values of one path are ORed. The event's name is the path `event`. For example: `{ event: ["workflow_run"], action: ["completed"], "workflow_run.conclusion": ["failure"], "workflow_run.head_branch": ["main"] }`.
- **A delivery that doesn't match** is answered 2xx, counted as `filtered` for the day, and never becomes a Fire. A Fire costs a Session and a Container, so filtering is not left to the agent; a generic dotted-path match covers GitHub, Linear and the rest without code for each. JSONPath and CEL were rejected as more than this needs.
- **As built, part 2:** the guard compares the body's `sender.login` with the App's bot login, `<slug>[bot]`, which the object asks of GitHub once as the App (`GET /app`, `GitHubApp.botLogin`) and only for a sender whose login ends `[bot]`; while GitHub can't say, any `[bot]` sender is dropped, since a loop costs more than a missed bot event: it fails closed, is counted as its own outcome `bot_unverified` (not `filtered`, so the owner can tell it from their own `match`) and a warning is logged.
- **A `match` that changes while a delivery waits:** the object checks a delivery against its cached definition and keeps the revision it passed (and, for a body over 1 MiB, the `facts` its `match` read). When the alarm finds the Registry's definition newer, it checks the delivery again against that one and counts one that no longer matches `filtered`, with no Session ([_The message's parts_](#payload-and-untrusted-content)).
- **The bot-loop guard:** in the `github` format, a delivery whose `sender` is the GitHub App's own bot is always dropped, so "on an issue comment" can't be answered by the agent's own comment, again and again. It has no switch; one could be added later.
- **The cached definition:** the object checks `match` against the definition it caches at a revision number, and `admitFire` refuses a stale revision (`stale`) and answers the current definition, which the object checks again. A cached `match` is never acted on wrongly.

## Overlap

_Each kind's in its slice: S1, S3, S4._

What a Fire does when the last one's work is still going on is the Trigger's `when_busy`:

| Kind                | `when_busy`              | Default                                      | Busy means                                                                                   |
| ------------------- | ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Environment trigger | `skip`, `start`          | `skip` for a Schedule, `start` for a webhook | the last Fire's Session is still running or waiting for the owner                            |
| Workspace trigger   | `wait`, `skip`, `start`  | `wait`                                       | any Session in the Workspace isn't idle, by the Registry's status (the owner's own included) |
| Session trigger     | `queue`, `steer`, `skip` | `queue`                                      | the Session's Run is going on                                                                |

- **`skip`** records the Fire `skipped: overlap`.
- **`start`** starts the Fire's Session anyway. For an Environment trigger's webhooks it is the default, since each delivery is its own event (one issue each).
- **`wait`** (Workspace trigger) keeps the Fire pending in the Trigger object: `admitFire` answers `busy`, and the object arms its alarm to try again a minute later. Two agents editing one tree at once is the hazard `wait` avoids; `skip` would lose webhook events, which GitHub won't redeliver.
- **`queue` and `steer`** (Session trigger) are `session_send`'s: `queue` waits behind the Run, `steer` joins it at its next boundary. A Run waiting for the owner (an Approval, an Elicitation request) is still going on, so `queue` waits behind it and `steer` joins after the owner answers; either is recorded `sent (queued)` or `sent (steered)`.
- **As built in S3** (`apps/server/src/trigger/object.ts` and `store.ts`; `TriggerWhenBusy` gains `wait`, `FireSkip` and `FireHeader` gain `coalesced`; `KIND_TRAITS` in `packages/protocol` gives each kind's `when_busy` values (the first is its default, `defaultWhenBusy`; `whenBusyProblem` refuses the rest, so only a Workspace trigger takes `wait`) and which options it takes (`takesBase`, `takesModel`, `ownOptionsProblem`)):
  - `admitFire` answers `busy` when any Session of the Workspace is running or waiting (the Registry's status), and the object keeps the Fire pending and retries a minute later; waiting counts no attempt, so it never gives up. The Fire's Session is registered in the existing Workspace (`workspace: <id>`). `wait` is refused for Environment triggers, `skip` and `start` work as for them.
  - Coalescing happens in the object's drain: while a Fire waits, a later one is recorded `coalesced` and counted (Trigger store migration 2, `trigger_pending.coalesced`, held as `PendingFire.waiting.merged`); it merges into the waiting Fire even if the Workspace has gone idle by then, and the started Session's header says how many more Fires came.
  - The object says what it waits for: its `state` has `waiting` (`since`, the Fire's queue time, and `merged`), which the owner's `listTriggers` passes on as `TriggerOverview.waiting` and the Settings row shows as "Waiting for the workspace to be idle (since …, N more merged)". `waiting` counts only a Fire not yet let through, as the drain does, and an older object omits it. A waiting Fire that fails for good (the Session refuses it, say) records its merged count in its fire-log entry (`failed: <reason>, 3 merged`), and one skipped for good (paused, or overlap after `when_busy` changed) in the outcome's `merged`, since those Fires are lost with it.
- **As built in S4** (`session/fire.ts`, `trigger/object.ts`): a Session trigger's Fire is the Session's `fireSession` (`SessionFireRequest`: the Fire's key, message, header and `whenBusy`, with no model or thinking, which the Session has), a method of its own beside `fire`, which an Environment trigger's Fire still uses, unchanged. An older Session has no `fireSession`, so the call fails loudly and the alarm tries it again with backoff, rather than an old `fire` starting a Run for a Fire that should have waited or been skipped. The outcome is `sent (started)` when the Session was idle, `sent (queued)` or `sent (steered)` otherwise; `skip` while a Run is going on records `skipped: overlap`. The answer is a `FireResult`: a `SubmitResult`, or `{skipped: overlap | coalesced}` when nothing was sent. _Retry caveat:_ a Fire retried after the owner moved the active branch (or after the Session's input was otherwise placed) may be sent a second time, as `submit` can be: the Session finds a request id on the active branch only.
- **Coalescing.** A Workspace trigger holds at most one waiting Fire; a later one is recorded `coalesced`, and the Session that finally starts is told how many more deliveries arrived while it waited, and to check the current state. A Session trigger sends no Fire while an earlier one of its own is still queued in the Session; the later one is recorded `coalesced`. Its Trigger message should say to check the current state. Merging queued messages was rejected: pi-durable's follow-up queue can't edit them. _As built:_ the Session keeps the Fire key of each Trigger's last send in its `trigger-last-fires` kv (trigger id → request id) and asks the host whether that input is still queued (`admitted(...).queued`), so a Fire a Run has taken up no longer coalesces the next.

## The Trigger object and the Registry

_Built in S1, with the hook in S5._

### Where it lives

- **`Trigger`**, keyed `trigger:<id>`, is in the `Server` Worker beside Session, Registry and `McpHost` (`SERVER_DURABLE_OBJECTS`, `worker-init.ts`'s `durableObjects`, and its code in the lazily loaded graph). Its class name can't be renamed once deployed.
- **It binds** the Registry and Session namespaces (same script), `GitHubAppConfig`, `SecretsConfig` (the vault) and telemetry. _As built_ (`apps/server/src/trigger/Trigger.ts`): S1 binds the Registry, the Sessions, the Workspace namespace (a Fire that took the spare runs the spare and Build upkeep, as the owner's start does) and telemetry; part 2 of S2 adds `SecretsConfig` (the vault) and `GitHubAppConfig` (the App's bot login); the GitHub hook comes with S5. `TriggerLive` needs `SessionLive`, so the Session reaches the Trigger namespace from the Worker's environment instead (`triggersOfEnvironment`, `trigger/objects.ts`), its stubs made as `yield* Trigger` makes them. The Session (for the tools) and the main Worker (for deliveries and the owner's UI) bind the Trigger namespace. A Worker of its own was rejected: it would duplicate the App's key and the vault, and add a step to the deploy order.
- **It follows `workers.md`:** its own migration manifest and shipped-migration test; tests with `refusingStorage` for a replaced instance; and an alarm that catches its own errors and re-arms, since the platform gives up after 6 retries.
- **Every edge only gains** ([`workers.md`](../development/workers.md) → _Deploys and Durable Object code updates_): main Worker → Trigger, Session → Trigger, Trigger → Registry (`admitFire`, `triggerDefinition`, `notifyTriggerFailed`, `knownTriggers`) and Trigger → Session (`fire`) are all new.

### Division of truth

| The Registry: _what_ a Trigger is and _whether_ it may fire                                                                                  | The Trigger object: _how_ it is reached and _when_ it fires     |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| its definition (kind, owner, name, Trigger message, source, `match`, `when_busy`, `base`, `model`, `thinking`, paused), at a revision number | its token and Signing secret, sealed                            |
| its Agent changes                                                                                                                            | its alarm and its pending Fires                                 |
| the status of the Sessions overlap depends on                                                                                                | its fire log and per-day counts                                 |
| the cascades of Session, Workspace and Environment deletion                                                                                  | its GitHub hook (S5) and the cached definition, at its revision |

### `admitFire`

The Registry's `admitFire` is the one place a Fire is let through. In one transaction it first answers a Fire it let through before (its key, the `requestId`) its Session again, whatever came since (a pause, a change, a removal, the overlap), so a retried Fire completes the Session it registered rather than leaving it empty. Otherwise it checks that the Trigger exists and isn't paused, that the caller's revision is current (else `stale`, with the current definition), and the overlap, which only the Registry can judge (the Sessions' statuses). For an Environment or Workspace trigger it also registers the Fire's Session (`createSession`) in the same transaction, so two Fires can't both pass an overlap check together. It answers the Session to send to (a new one, or the Session trigger's own), `busy` (a Workspace trigger's `wait`), or why the Fire is skipped. _As built in S4:_ a Session trigger's answer is its own status, `send` (with its Session and Environment; `repeated` for a Fire let through before), given only to a request that says `sessionTriggers: true`, which an older Trigger object would not send: it would read `started` with an `existing` flag it ignores and run the Environment's upkeep. For a request that does not say so, `admitFire` answers `unsupported` inside the Registry, which fails the call, so the older object tries the Fire again once it is replaced. No Session is registered and no upkeep follows. The Session is the trigger's `triggers.session` (a column of migration 38, with an index); one that is gone fails the Fire for good (`its Session is gone`). A Fire let through is remembered 24 hours in `trigger_sends` (request id → Session, also migration 38, with an index on its Session, which a Session's deletion uses: no Session was made for it, so `session_requests` cannot hold it), and answered again before the Trigger's pause, revision or existence is looked at, as an Environment trigger's is, so an admitted Fire goes on while its Trigger is paused or removed, as long as its Session is listed. The rows go with the Session's Triggers (`deleteSessionTriggers` and a Session's deletion delete them too), so a repeat of a Fire admitted just before the Triggers went answers `removed` and not `send`; `registered` (below) covers the other half: a `fireSession` call already in flight when the Triggers went, admitted before they did, which `admitFire` is not asked about again. It counts nothing and caps nothing: there is no Trigger fire limit, no cap on trigger-started Sessions running at once, and no count of Triggers per Session or Environment.

### Pending Fires

- **One path for both sources.** A Schedule coming due and an accepted delivery both become a pending Fire in the object's storage, which its alarm drains: `admitFire`, then the Session's `fire`. There is no cap on pending Fires; coalescing bounds what waits, and `start` drains at once.
- **The Fire's key** is `<trigger id>:<scheduled time>` for a Schedule, or `<trigger id>:<delivery id>` for a webhook delivery, and is the `requestId` of `createSession` and of `fire`. `createSession` remembers a `requestId` for 24 hours (`REQUEST_MEMORY_MS`) and the Session dedupes its `fire`, so a retried alarm never starts a second Session or sends twice. A redelivery GitHub is asked for a day later fires again, which is right: the object holds a delivery's key against a repeat for 24 hours (`DEDUPE_MS`: pending, or in the fire log within the day), as long as the Registry's memory of it. A delivery's key starts with the Trigger's id, never the sender's delivery id alone, because `requestId`s are global to the Registry and the Sessions: a `standard` `webhook-id` can repeat across Triggers, and a sender that holds one Trigger's Signing secret must not be able to choose another Trigger's key and so answer, or block, its Fires.
- **`fire`** is a new Session method, never an optional field on `submit`: a Session still on old code (for up to 300 s after a deploy) drops fields it doesn't know and would take a payload as the owner's own words, unfenced. With a new method an old Session fails loudly, and the alarm retries until the new code is there.
- **Retries, as built** (`apps/server/src/trigger/object.ts`): a Fire whose `admitFire` or `fire` can't be reached, or whose `fire` answers a retryable error, is tried again a minute later, the wait doubling to at most an hour; after 12 tries (some 7 hours) it is recorded `failed` and pushed. A `fire` refused for good is recorded `failed` and pushed at once. An alarm that can't read its definition re-arms a minute later and touches nothing. A `stale` answer is adopted and admitted again, at most 3 times in one alarm. The spare and Build upkeep run at a first `started` admission, before the Session's `fire`, since the spare was taken then.
- **An admitted Fire is completed, as built.** `admitFire` answers a Fire it let through before (its `requestId`) its Session again before it looks at the Trigger, so a retry after a pause, a change or a removal sends the message to the Session it registered rather than leaving that Session empty. The object keeps the Session `admitFire` answered beside the pending Fire (`trigger_pending.session`): a pause keeps the admitted ones (and arms the alarm for them alone) and records the others `skipped: paused`; a removal (the Registry no longer reads the definition, or a caller's `remove()`) tries each admitted one once more, then wipes the object, logging each pending Fire it drops (`failed: removed`: the fire log goes with the object).
- **The Session's `fire`, as built** (`session/fire.ts`): it goes in through the endpoint's send (`deliver`, which `submit` shares), so its archived and importing refusals use the endpoint's wording, a Fire a Stop withdrew answers `stopped`, and Fire keys share the 64 request ids whose delivery the Session keeps.
- **Fire now, as built:** the object's `fireNow` keeps a pending Fire keyed `<trigger>:manual:<uuid>`, recorded `manual`, and sets its alarm for now; a paused Trigger refuses it (`paused`). The Schedule's cursor is untouched.

### Self-healing

At each alarm the object reads its definition again (`triggerDefinition`). A Trigger that was removed retires the object: its admitted Fires get one last try, then it is wiped (its hook deleted first, S5). One paused drops its Fires not yet admitted, recorded `skipped: paused`, and stays armed only for those already admitted, which complete. A changed Schedule re-arms it. So a lost "re-arm" from a caller heals at the object's next touch, as the `McpHost` sync does. A delivery to a Trigger the Registry no longer knows heals the object the same way. _As built:_ a read (`state`, `fireLog`) of an object with no storage, never synced or wiped, answers empty without opening its store, so a read never makes a removed Trigger's tables again.

### Cascades

- **As built in S1:** deleting an Environment runs the Registry's `deleteEnvironmentWithTriggers`, which deletes its Triggers in the same transaction and answers their ids; `removals` (`registry/removal.ts`, which the owner's delete and `environment_delete` both run) then wipes each object (`TriggerObjects.remove`, `trigger/objects.ts`), logging a wipe that fails. The owner's removal deletes the definition outright; the agent's keeps it for a revert ([_Agent tools and Approval_](#agent-tools-and-approval)). `environment_delete`'s Approval card counts the Environment's Triggers with what else the delete takes, and an Approval asked before they were counted covers only an Environment that has none.
- **As built in S4:** a Session's deletion removes its Session triggers _before_ its object is wiped: `removals.deleteSession` calls the Registry's `deleteSessionTriggers` (the rows, answering their ids), wipes each Trigger's object, then wipes the Session's object and forgets the Session (`deleteSessionWithTriggers`, which deletes any Trigger made since, in the same transaction as the record, and answers its id). In the other order a Fire could be admitted between the wipe and the record's deletion (indefinitely, when a Subagent Workspace's removal fails) and start a Run, which spends money, in an object nothing lists; a Session that is kept after a failed delete is left without its Session triggers. The Session's `fireSession` also refuses for good (`not_found`, recorded `failed`) when the Registry no longer lists the Session (`registered`). That covers only a `fireSession` call already in flight, admitted before the Triggers went: the object's last try for an admitted Fire (`retire`) is a repeat that goes through `admitFire` again, whose `trigger_sends` rows went with the Triggers, so it answers `removed` and the object wipes itself; a Registry that can't say fails the call, which is tried again. An import that expired deletes its Session from inside its own alarm (`removeOwnSession`), the record first and the wipe last. Fork and Clone create a new Session row and copy no trigger (the Registry registers the new Session by its own id; triggers hang off `triggers.session`).
- **Deleting a Session, removing a Workspace or deleting an Environment:** the Registry deletes the Triggers it holds (a Session's Session triggers; a Workspace's Workspace triggers; an Environment's all three kinds, through its Workspaces and Sessions) in the same transaction, and returns their ids. The caller (the main Worker's `deleteSession` and `removals`, which `environment_delete` runs too) then calls each object's `remove()`, which deletes its GitHub hook, its alarm and its storage.
- **As built in S3** (`registry/store.ts`, `registry/triggers.ts`, `registry/removal.ts`): a Workspace trigger's definition holds `workspace: { id, branch }` (`TriggerDefinition.workspace`; the input is `WorkspaceTriggerInput`, `kind: "workspace"`, in the `TriggerInput` union, with the same sources as an Environment trigger's: `defaultWhenBusy(kind, source)` gives a Workspace trigger `wait` whatever its source), and Registry migration 35 adds its column. The agent's `trigger_create` and the Workspaces row's New trigger made Schedule Workspace triggers only; the webhook arguments reached the tools in part 3 of #679 and the form in part 4. The Registry refuses a Workspace that is missing, spare or a Subagent Workspace.
  - **The lifetime rule** is the Registry's. `store.workspaceKept(id)` (the Registry's `workspaceKept`) says whether an active Workspace trigger is kept for a Workspace; `removeIfEmpty`, run after a Session's delete, asks it once and leaves a kept Workspace. `store.deleteWorkspace` answers `in-use` while a Session or a Workspace trigger is bound, and `deleteWorkspaceWithTriggers` (in the store and in the Registry, one operation) is the owner's Remove, which deletes the Triggers with the record instead.
  - **A Workspace that loses its last Workspace trigger while it has no Session stays**, listed with no Session and no trigger, until the owner removes it from the Workspaces section: it is not removed automatically, since it may hold unpushed work.
  - **The owner's Remove takes the Triggers first.** `removeWorkspace` runs `deleteWorkspaceTriggers` and wipes their objects before it deletes the Workspace's Sessions, so a waiting Fire can't start a Session in a Workspace being torn down; the sweep's last step, `deleteWorkspaceWithTriggers`, also takes one made meanwhile. A failure after the Triggers went (a Session that can't be wiped, say) says they are already removed, so the owner sees what is left. Environment delete runs the same `removeWorkspace` per Workspace.
  - **A Workspace trigger the agent removed** is deleted with its Workspace (its row is kept for a revert only while the Workspace exists), so a revert afterwards says it was deleted for good.
  - **`removeIfEmpty`'s race:** a Session started in the Workspace, or a Workspace trigger made for it, between the check and the record's deletion keeps the record (`deleteWorkspace` answers `in-use`), but not the files its object just wiped: they are made again as a Session first works there. The warning says so.
- **A failed hook delete** re-arms the alarm, with backoff, for up to a day, then logs a warning.
- **A lost `remove()`** heals at the object's next touch ([_Self-healing_](#self-healing)).
- **A removal the agent made** keeps the definition in the Registry, as removed, while its Agent change is in the Scope's 200-entry history, so a revert brings it back (with a new URL and secret: [_Secrets_](#secrets)).

## GitHub-managed hooks

_Built in S5 (#682), after S0 is deployed and S2; the owner's accept is S6's._

- **The hook.** For a webhook Trigger in the `github` format whose source is a GitHub repo and a list of events, the Trigger object creates a repo webhook as the GitHub App, with a token asking only `{ repository_hooks: write }`, minted for the installation on the repo's owner: subscribed only to the Trigger's events, sending to the stage's own Trigger URL, signed with the Trigger's Signing secret. It updates the hook when the owner replaces either value, and deletes it when the Trigger goes. A reverted removal makes it again. The agent wires up "when CI fails on `main`, fix it" with nothing for the owner to paste.
- **The orphan sweep.** Before it creates a hook in a repo, the object lists that repo's hooks and deletes those that point at this stage's `/hooks/<id>/` and whose id the Registry doesn't know (`knownTriggers(ids)`). A destroyed preview's hooks point at a dead host and fail harmlessly at GitHub until then; there is no CI sweep, and S6 removes its own Triggers.
- **GitHub's limits.** At most 20 hooks per event per repo: GitHub's refusal is shown plainly, and cloudpid sets no cap of its own.
- **The permission.** The App gains the repository permission Webhooks: write (`repository_hooks`). `GITHUB_APP_PERMISSIONS`, the App's list, gains it for new registrations; for the existing App the owner adds it in the App's settings and accepts it on each installation. An installation that hasn't accepted it gets a clear message naming that step, in the tool's result and on the Trigger's page.
- **Container tokens** (S0, built) are minted with an explicit list of permissions, the Containers' list (`CONTAINER_GITHUB_PERMISSIONS`), kept in `@cloudpid/config` beside the App's list (`GITHUB_APP_PERMISSIONS`), which `infra` and `server` share: the Workspace DO's `GitHubApp` (`apps/server/src/workspace/Workspace.ts`) always passes it, so every GitHub token request carries `permissions`. Before S0 it asked for none, which GitHub reads as all of the installation's. The two lists are separate and hold the same values until S5, which adds `repository_hooks` to the App's list only; it is never in the Containers' list (a test fails if it is). Without the list, a prompt-injected `gh api` could add a hook that sends a repo's events anywhere. S0 deploys before the owner accepts the permission; tokens minted before it expire within the hour. Any permission the App gains later reaches Containers only when it is added to their list, deliberately.
- **The App's own webhook** stays off ([ADR-0049](../adr/0049-github-triggers-are-repo-webhooks-the-github-app-makes.md)).
- **As built in S5** (`apps/server/src/trigger/github-hook.ts`, `hook-sync.ts`, `object.ts`, `store.ts`; `apps/server/src/registry/trigger-features.ts`; `packages/protocol/src/triggers.ts`, `trigger-github.ts`):
  - **The definition.** A `github` webhook source takes optional `repo` (`owner/name`) and `events` (each a GitHub event's name, `[a-z_]`, no `*`, each once), both or neither (`githubHookProblem`, the Registry's refusal and the tools' and form's alike); they are fixed at creation, as the format is, so a patch keeps them. They are fields of the webhook source, not a source type of their own: the Registry's RPC input is not decoded, so an older Registry would keep a new type as silently as it drops the fields. A caller therefore offers them only once the Registry's `triggerFeatures()` answers `githubHooks: true`, and refuses while it is missing or false ("the server is being updated", nothing saved); the Session's `trigger_create` and the owner's `createTrigger` both ask it through `githubHookRefusal` (`registry/trigger-features.ts`), which reads any other failure of the call as no answer: logged, and refused as unknown ("could not say … try again"), never as a deploy underway ([`workers.md`](../development/workers.md)). A new create method that an older Registry would fail loudly was rejected: it would double the create's owner, agent and Approval paths, where one question before the one create keeps them single. `repo` is `owner/name` as GitHub names them: an owner that starts with a letter or digit, and never a repo named `.` or `..`.
  - **The client** (`githubRepoHooks`) lists, makes, updates and deletes a repo's hooks as the GitHub App, its token minted with `{ repository_hooks: write }` alone (`TRIGGER_HOOK_PERMISSIONS`) for the installation on the repo's owner, never the App owner's. Each call is a `trigger.github_hook` span. What GitHub refuses is a `GitHubHookFailed` with a code the object and the owner act on (`GitHubHookProblemCode`): `permission` (a mint answered 422, or a hooks call 403 "Resource not accessible": the message names the App setting and the installation's accept), `not_installed`, `refused` (GitHub's own words: the 20 hooks per event among them), `unreachable`, `no_origin` (`alchemy dev`, whose Trigger URLs GitHub can't reach, makes none) and `unknown` for a code a later version adds.
  - **The object** keeps the hook in `trigger_github_hook` (migration 4: the repo, GitHub's hook id, a fingerprint of what it was set with — the stage's origin, the token's keyed hash and the sealed secret — the last problem, the tries and the next try's time) and the hooks still to delete in `trigger_github_deletes`. Each adopt reconciles: a hook missing or set with an old fingerprint is updated (one GitHub no longer has is made again), and only when there is none to update is the repo swept and a hook made. A failure is kept with its problem, and tried again after 1 minute, doubling to an hour, for as long as the Trigger exists; the alarm is armed for it. A sync (any change of the Trigger, a pause or resume, a revert) and a rotation try at once; a rotation updates the hook before it answers. `state` and `reveal` say where the hook is (`GitHubHookStatus`: `ready`, `pending`, `failed` with the problem), and the owner's `listTriggers` passes it on as `TriggerOverview.github`.
  - **The sync's rules** (`hookSync`, `hook-sync.ts`, which the object calls at each adopt, rotation, wipe and alarm): a definition that names no hook leaves a kept one where it is, logged, and only one that names another repo moves it (the old hook to the deletes): a definition read from an older Registry, after a rollback, has no `repo`, and must not delete working hooks. A forced reconcile (a sync after a change, a rotation) forgets the App's last failure to mint for the repo's owner (`GitHubApp.forget`, through `RepoHooks.forgetFailure`), since the owner may just have accepted the permission and the cached refusal would otherwise be told again for its minutes. A Trigger whose definition names a hook the object has no row for (an object older than its definition, say) arms its alarm for now, so it heals without waiting for a touch.
  - **The sweep** runs only before a hook is made, never on a schedule: of the repo's hooks that send to this stage's `/hooks/<id>/`, those of a Trigger the Registry doesn't know (`knownTriggers(ids)`, which counts active and paused Triggers and not those the agent removed, kept for a revert) and any at this Trigger's own id (a hook it made and lost) are deleted. When the Registry can't be asked, no other Trigger's hook is.
  - **Deletes.** A wipe (the Trigger removed, its Environment or Workspace deleted) deletes the hook first; one that fails is kept, the wipe's other storage gone in the same transaction (`wipeKeepingDeletes`, so an eviction never leaves a half-wiped object or loses a delete), and tried again by the alarm, with backoff, for a day, then given up with a warning naming the repo's settings. A paused Trigger keeps its hook, so a resume needs nothing at GitHub.
  - **The permission.** `GITHUB_APP_PERMISSIONS` gains `repository_hooks: write`, and `CONTAINER_GITHUB_PERMISSIONS` never has it: both are held by `packages/config/src/github-permissions.test.ts`. A Container's mint asks for the Containers' list (`github-token.workerd.test.ts`), so it never carries the permission.
  - **The trimmer** (`trimGitHubBody` over `trimGitHubJson`, `packages/protocol/src/trigger-github.ts`): before a GitHub Fire's body is fenced inline, every `*_url` key at any depth but `html_url` is dropped, and `repository`, `sender`, `organization` and `installation` shrink to their `full_name`, `login` or `id` (to `null` when one has no such field); an urlencoded `payload=` is read as its JSON. The fenced block says `trimmed="true"`; the file in the Workspace keeps the body as delivered.

## Agent tools and Approval

_Built in S1; the owners `workspace` and `session` in S3 and S4, the webhook arguments and `trigger_match_set` in part 3 of #679, and the GitHub-managed hook's `repo` and `events` in S5._

- **Tools,** one purpose each ([ADR-0019](../adr/0019-the-agent-manages-its-environments-and-settings.md)): `trigger_list`, `trigger_get`, `trigger_create`, `trigger_message_set`, `trigger_schedule_set`, `trigger_match_set`, `trigger_options_set`, `trigger_pause`, `trigger_resume` and `trigger_remove`. They are deferred and the root conversation's only (`trigger_` joins `PARENT_ONLY_TOOL_PREFIXES`). Each change tool takes a required `reason` and the Trigger's id.
- **`trigger_create`** names the Trigger's owner explicitly: an Environment's id, `workspace` (this Session's Workspace; the agent has no tool that lists other Workspaces, so it can only mean "here", and taking a Workspace id later is additive) or `session` (this Session). It takes the name, the Trigger message, the source (`schedule` with its zone, or `webhook: {format, repo?, events?}`, exactly one of them; `repo` and `events` for a GitHub-managed hook), and optionally `match` (a webhook's only), `when_busy`, `base` (Environment triggers), `model` and `thinking`. The Registry refuses a Subagent Workspace and a spare.
- **`trigger_match_set`** changes which deliveries a webhook Trigger takes: `{id, match, reason}`, where `match` is the new match (dotted paths of the body, each with the values it may have) or `null` for none (an empty one is the same). It waits for Approval, and refuses a Schedule Trigger, as `trigger_schedule_set` refuses a webhook one, without asking. Neither tool changes the source's type or format: to move a Trigger to the other, remove it and make another.
- **`trigger_options_set`** changes `when_busy`, `base`, `model` and `thinking`, each where the Trigger's kind takes it (`base` for Environment triggers only, `model` and `thinking` for Environment and Workspace triggers, `when_busy` with its kind's values: [_Overlap_](#overlap)).
- **Kind and owner are fixed** at creation: a Trigger's kind and its Environment, Workspace or Session never change. To move one, remove it and create another.
- **No secrets in results.** A webhook Trigger's card, result and outputs never hold its URL or Signing secret, and no tool can read or rotate them (`reveal` and `rotate` are the owner's RPCs alone); they say the two are the user's, on the Trigger's page in Settings, and tell the agent to ask the user to set up the sender there. A `match` is kept to plain text as a message is: one holding a Secret the Session holds, a GitHub credential or a Secret Reference is refused.
- **No fire-now tool.** The agent can't start Sessions at will today (`environment_create`'s result says so), and an approved Trigger must not become a way to.
- **Agent changes.** Every Trigger change is an Agent change, recorded and revertible, under the Environment's Scope with the field `triggers.<id>`. A Workspace trigger's and a Session trigger's are recorded under their Environment's Scope, naming the Workspace by its branch or the Session.
- **Approval** ([ADR-0047](../adr/0047-triggers-fire-from-a-durable-object-of-their-own-the-registry-keeps-their-definitions-and-making-or-widening-one-waits-for-approval.md)): `trigger_create`, any change to the Trigger message, Schedule, `match` or options (`trigger_message_set`, `trigger_schedule_set`, `trigger_match_set`, `trigger_options_set`), and `trigger_resume` wait for the owner's Approval. `trigger_pause` and `trigger_remove` apply at once: they only narrow, as for MCP server changes. The owner's own edits never wait.
- **As built in S1** (`packages/extensions/src/triggers.ts`, over the Session's `TriggerAccess`, `apps/server/src/session/triggers.ts`; the Registry's side in `apps/server/src/registry/trigger-changes.ts`):
  - `trigger_create` takes only an Environment's id as `owner` (S3 added `workspace`, S4 adds `session`). All nine tools are deferred; `trigger_` is in `PARENT_ONLY_TOOL_PREFIXES`. (`trigger_match_set` and the webhook source came in part 3, below.)
  - Each tool refuses without asking what the Registry would refuse (a Schedule without a zone, 6- or 7-field, with `L`, `W` or `#`; a name or message out of bounds; an unknown Environment, Global or a Trigger id it doesn't hold) and a reason, name or message that holds a Secret the Session holds, a GitHub credential or a Secret Reference. A Trigger message has no bound of the agent's own: the card shows its first 16,000 characters (as a Skill's card shows its files, `MAX_CARD_CHARS`), and a longer one is shown cut, between two warnings, with a note that the rest is not reviewed. A change to the values the Trigger has asks nothing and records nothing; it still reaches the Registry, which answers `unchanged`.
  - The card shows the Trigger as the owner reads it (name, Schedule, options, message) and, for a Schedule, its next 3 Fires in its zone in `notes`. `expected` is the Trigger as its Agent changes record it (`TriggerChangeValue`), and an approved change is made only while the Trigger still is that (`changed-since` otherwise; a rerun after the change went through finds the values there and says it was done). `trigger_create` draws the Trigger's id before asking and keeps it as `expected`, so a rerun after an eviction makes that Trigger, once.
  - The Registry records each change (`agentCreateTrigger`, `agentUpdateTrigger`, `agentSetTriggerPaused`, `agentRemoveTrigger`) as the field `triggers.<id>` under the Trigger's Environment, `before` and `after` the Trigger's `TriggerChangeValue` (its kind, name, message, source, options and paused; null before its creation and after its removal). The agent's removal sets `removed_at`, taking the Trigger out of every read and every Fire; its row is dropped once no change in the Environment's history (its last 200) refers to it. The owner's revert removes a Trigger the agent made, brings back one it removed (active or paused as it was, at a new revision) and puts a change's values back, refused once the Trigger is no longer as the change left it.
  - After each call the Registry answered `changed` or `unchanged` the Session wakes the Trigger's object, and after the owner's revert the main Worker does, so the object arms, re-arms, disarms or wipes itself. An `unchanged` answer wakes it too: it may be the rerun of a call cut off after its change and before its wake. A Trigger made or resumed has no alarm to heal from, so its object is tried 3 more times (`TriggerObjects.wake`, 0.2 s apart and doubling), and then the tool fails, saying the change is saved and asking for `trigger_resume`, which asks nothing for an active Trigger and wakes it again. The owner's revert that leaves an active Trigger that had no alarm (bringing back one the agent removed, or undoing its pause; `armsTrigger`) wakes it the same way, and when the object never answers the revert stays made and answers an error asking the owner to pause and resume the Trigger. After any other change or revert, a wake that fails is logged (`TriggerObjects.sync`) and left to the object's next alarm.

## Fire records, origin marks and notifications

_Built in S1, the delivery counts in S2 (part 2 keeps them; the owner's page shows them, part 4)._

- **The fire log** is a row per Fire in its Trigger object: when, the source (the scheduled time; the delivery id and event; or `manual`), and the outcome: `started <session>`, `sent (started)`, `sent (queued)`, `sent (steered)`, `skipped: overlap | coalesced | archived | importing | missed | paused` (S1 adds `paused`: a Fire not yet admitted when its Trigger was paused), or `failed: <reason>`. The last 1,000 Fires are kept, and the per-day counts of refused and filtered deliveries for 30 days; both go with the Trigger.
- **Origin marks.** `MarkedOrigin`'s `auth` gains the member `trigger`, holding the Trigger's id and name; an older reader decodes it as `unknown` rather than failing. _As built:_ its `kind` gains `trigger` too, and `trigger: {id, name}` sits beside them (`packages/protocol/src/mcp-api.ts`); an input a Fire sent carries `via: {auth: "trigger", trigger}`. A Session a Trigger started shows the chip "Trigger · `<name>`", and each input a Trigger sent shows "via `<name>` (Trigger)".
- **Notifications.** A Run a Trigger started pushes `finished` or `failed` at its end, as the owner's submissions do: `fire` records its submission in `notify-submissions`, as MCP task follow-ups are. A Fire that fails pushes. Overlap and coalesced skips, filtered deliveries and refused ones never push. `client-and-protocol.md` → Notifications' **Triggers** bullet is renamed **When they go out**, so the word means one thing.

## Observability

_Each span with the slice that builds its code._

Attributes are fixed values and counts only: never the token, the Signing secret, the body, or a header's value other than the event's name and the delivery id. Paths are recorded as the route template, `/hooks/:id/:token`.

- **`trigger.delivery`** (built in part 2; the Worker's `hooks.delivery` span, with the route template and the status, is its parent): the Trigger's id (`trigger.id`), format, outcome (`accepted`, `filtered`, `bot_unverified`, `unauthorized`, `too_large`, `unsupported`, `not_found`, `ping`), byte count, event. The Trigger's id is no secret (the token is); the Worker's span never has it.
- **`trigger.fire`:** Trigger id, kind (`environment`, `workspace` or `session`), source (`schedule`, `webhook`, `manual`), outcome (`started`, `sent`, `skipped:<why>`, `failed`), lag behind the scheduled time in ms, the alarm's retry count.
- **`trigger.alarm`.**
- **As built in S1** (`trigger/object.ts`): `trigger.fire` also records `trigger.attempts`, the tries the pending Fire had before this one, and its outcome may be `retry` (the Fire waits for a later try: what most often follows an outage, which `failed` alone would hide); `trigger.alarm` records `trigger.id`. Both are custom spans of the Worker's trace, under the object's own tracer (`Trigger.ts`).
- **`trigger.github_hook`:** `create`, `update`, `delete` or `sweep`, its outcome and HTTP status.
- **Telemetry names:** `trigger.*`. The `mcp.trigger` span attribute and "Other triggers (D7.2)" in `mcp-connections.md` are internal or prose, and stay.
